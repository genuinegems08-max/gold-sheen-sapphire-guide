Gold Sheen Sapphire is one of the more durable coloured gems you can own — but
"durable" is not the same as "indestructible," and the very inclusions that create
its sheen call for a gentle cleaning routine. This guide covers everyday wear, safe
cleaning, storage, and the few things to avoid. For a quick, task-focused routine,
see [cleaning Gold Sheen Sapphire jewellery](/guide/cleaning-gold-sheen-sapphire-jewelry/).

<KeyTakeaways>
- At **Mohs 9**, it is highly scratch-resistant and suits **daily wear**, including rings.
- Clean with **warm water, mild soap and a soft brush** — nothing more.
- **Avoid ultrasonic and steam cleaners**, sudden temperature changes, and harsh chemicals.
- **Store it separately** so it neither scratches nor gets scratched.
- Have the **setting** checked yearly — most lost stones are worn prongs, not gem failure.
</KeyTakeaways>

## How durable is it, really?

As corundum, Gold Sheen Sapphire sits at **9 on the Mohs hardness scale**, second
only to diamond. In practical terms:

- It resists scratching from almost everything it meets in daily life — dust, keys,
  other jewellery.
- It has good toughness, but any gem can chip or fracture under a sharp,
  concentrated blow, especially at an exposed edge.
- Because it contains a dense inclusion field, sudden thermal shock and high-energy
  cleaning are worth avoiding.

That combination makes it excellent for rings and everyday pieces, with a little
common-sense protection.

## Everyday wear

- **Put jewellery on last, take it off first.** Apply lotions, perfume and
  hairspray before your rings go on; residues dull the polished surface the sheen
  depends on.
- **Remove it for rough work.** Gardening, gym sessions, DIY and heavy cleaning are
  when knocks and chemicals happen.
- **Mind the setting, not just the stone.** Most "lost stone" problems are worn
  prongs or loosened bezels, not gem failure — see [Jewellery &
  settings](/guide/gold-sheen-sapphire-jewelry-and-settings/).

## Cleaning — the safe method

For a stone whose beauty is a surface reflection, a clean, smear-free surface is
everything.

1. Fill a small bowl with **warm (not hot) water** and a little **mild dish soap**.
2. Soak the piece for a few minutes to loosen residues.
3. Gently work around the stone and setting with a **soft brush** (a soft
   toothbrush is ideal).
4. Rinse in clean water — hold it over a bowl, not an open drain.
5. Dry and buff with a **soft, lint-free cloth**.

That's all it needs. A clean stone shows its sheen at its best.

## Do and don't, at a glance

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Warm water + mild soap + soft brush | Ultrasonic or steam cleaners |
| Dry with a soft, lint-free cloth | Boiling water or sudden temperature swings |
| Store each piece separately | Toss it loose in a jewellery drawer |
| Remove for sport, DIY and cleaning | Expose it to bleach, chlorine or solvents |
| Check the setting once a year | Scrub with abrasive cloths or pastes |

<Callout type="warning" title="Why not ultrasonic?">
Ultrasonic and steam cleaning are fine for many clean, faceted stones, but Gold
Sheen Sapphire's beauty comes from a **dense inclusion field**. High-energy or
thermal cleaning is an unnecessary risk when warm soapy water does the job safely.
</Callout>

## Storage

- Store each piece **separately** — a soft pouch, or a lined box compartment.
- Keep it away from softer gems and metals it could scratch (its Mohs 9 will mark
  almost anything else).
- Avoid loose "jewellery drawer" jumbles where stones knock together.

## Long-term care and check-ups

Once a year, or after any hard knock, have the **setting** checked by a jeweller —
prongs wear, bezels loosen, and catching a problem early prevents a lost stone. A
professional can also re-polish the metal and refresh the piece without any
aggressive treatment to the gem itself.
