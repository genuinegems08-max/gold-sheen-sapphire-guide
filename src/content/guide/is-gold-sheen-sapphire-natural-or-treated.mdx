Among coloured stones, sapphire is one of the most routinely treated — so a natural,
untreated sapphire variety is worth understanding carefully. The short answer:
**Gold Sheen Sapphire is characteristically natural and unheated.** This article
explains why, and how it's confirmed. It supports the pillar, [what Gold Sheen
Sapphire is](/guide/what-is-gold-sheen-sapphire/), and the [buying
guide](/guide/gold-sheen-sapphire-buying-guide/).

<KeyTakeaways>
- The sheen depends on **delicate oriented inclusions** that heat would tend to destroy.
- So the material is valued specifically in its **natural, unheated** state.
- Laboratory studies report minerals (**boehmite, diaspore, kaolinite**) consistent with **no heat treatment**.
- The phenomenon is **structural**, never a coating, dye or diffusion.
- For a significant purchase, get an **independent report** confirming natural and unheated.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Why heat would work against the sheen

Most sapphire heat treatment aims to improve *transparent* colour and clarity — and
one of its side effects is dissolving or disrupting internal "silk." But in Gold
Sheen Sapphire, that silk **is** the product: the oriented hematite–ilmenite network
is what creates the sheen (see [how it
forms](/guide/how-gold-sheen-sapphire-forms/)). Heat the stone and you would risk
degrading the very feature that gives it value. There is simply no incentive to
treat it — and every incentive to keep it natural.

## What the laboratories find

Independent studies of material from the deposit describe an intact, oriented
inclusion network, and — importantly — associated minerals that would not survive
high-temperature treatment. A GIT study of sheen and non-sheen sapphires from the
same Kenyan locality reported the presence of **boehmite, diaspore and kaolinite**,
which it noted are "good indications of untreated stones."[^1] GIA's laboratory has
likewise documented the characteristic hematite–ilmenite inclusions in the
material.[^2]

<Callout type="key" title="Not a coating, dye, or diffusion">
The sheen is not applied to the surface and cannot be. It is a reflection from
inclusions **inside** the crystal, formed as the sapphire cooled. That's why it
moves with the light and why it can't be imitated with a surface treatment.
</Callout>

## What to ask before you buy

- Request an **independent laboratory report** — not just the seller's own paperwork.
- The report should confirm **natural corundum** and, ideally, note **no indication
  of heating**.
- Learn to see the [genuine inclusion field
  yourself](/guide/how-to-spot-fake-gold-sheen-sapphire/), so you understand what the
  report is describing.
