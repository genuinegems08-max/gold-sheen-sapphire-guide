At a glance, a golden, silky sheen can make two very different stones look alike.
**Gold Sheen Sapphire** and **tiger's eye** are sometimes confused — but they are
different minerals, with very different hardness, rarity and value. This article
draws the line clearly. It sits under [Gold Sheen Sapphire vs other
sapphires](/guide/gold-sheen-sapphire-vs-other-sapphires/).

<KeyTakeaways>
- **Gold Sheen Sapphire** = corundum (sapphire), **Mohs 9**, from one depleted deposit.
- **Tiger's eye** = a fibrous **quartz**, **Mohs ~7**, mined abundantly worldwide.
- Both show a golden sheen, but tiger's eye's is a fine, **fibrous banding**; the sapphire's is a **metallic schiller** from oriented oxide platelets.
- The value gap is large: a rare unheated sapphire versus a common, inexpensive quartz.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Same look, different minerals

Tiger's eye is a quartz whose chatoyancy comes from fine, parallel **fibres**
(historically explained as replaced crocidolite). Gold Sheen Sapphire is **corundum**
whose sheen comes from oriented **hematite–ilmenite platelets** (see [what it
is](/guide/what-is-gold-sheen-sapphire/)). The effects can rhyme visually, but the
materials could hardly be more different.

## Side by side

| | Gold Sheen Sapphire | Tiger's Eye |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mineral | Corundum (sapphire) | Quartz |
| Mohs hardness | 9 | ~7 |
| Cause of sheen | Oriented hematite–ilmenite platelets | Parallel fibres |
| Look of the sheen | Metallic golden schiller | Silky fibrous bands |
| Source & supply | Single depleted Kenyan deposit | Abundant, worldwide |
| Relative value | High (rare, unheated sapphire) | Low (common) |

## How to tell them apart

- **Hardness (for professionals):** corundum is markedly harder than quartz, though
  you should never scratch-test a stone you're evaluating.
- **The sheen's character:** tiger's eye shows fine, even fibrous banding; Gold Sheen
  Sapphire shows a broader, more metallic band of golden light.
- **Price and provenance:** tiger's eye is inexpensive and everywhere; a genuine Gold
  Sheen Sapphire is sold with — or can be confirmed by — an [independent
  report](/guide/how-to-spot-fake-gold-sheen-sapphire/).

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick check">
If it's cheap and abundant, it's tiger's eye. A genuine Gold Sheen Sapphire is a
rare, hard, unheated sapphire — and priced accordingly.
</Callout>
