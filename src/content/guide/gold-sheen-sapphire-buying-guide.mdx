Buying a phenomenal gem is different from buying a transparent one. You are not
grading a stone for what light passes *through* it, but for what light does
*across* it. This guide gives you a repeatable way to judge Gold Sheen Sapphire —
sheen, colour, cut, size, and certification — and to understand what you're paying
for. For the underlying science, see [what Gold Sheen Sapphire
is](/guide/what-is-gold-sheen-sapphire/).

<KeyTakeaways>
- **Grade the sheen first** — strength, coverage, continuity and direction matter more than size.
- Body colour is judged by **contrast** with the sheen, not by a "best" hue.
- Cut quality is about **orientation, dome height, symmetry and polish**.
- There is **no fixed per-carat price**; value is a combination of factors against a fixed, depleted supply.
- For any significant purchase, insist on an **independent report** confirming natural, unheated corundum.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Step 1 — Grade the sheen first

The sheen is the reason the stone exists, so it leads the assessment. Rock the
cabochon slowly under a single light source and watch four things:

- **Strength:** how bright and obvious is the band of light? A strong, luminous
  sheen commands a premium.
- **Coverage:** does the sheen cross the *whole* dome, or only part of it? Even,
  full coverage is best.
- **Continuity:** does the band travel smoothly, or break up and stutter?
- **Direction:** a well-oriented stone shows the sheen crossing the face cleanly,
  not running off an edge.

A stone with strong, even, well-oriented sheen outranks a larger stone with a
patchy one. Train your eye by comparing several stones side by side.

### A working sheen-grade scale

There is no universal grading lab scale for the sheen, so use a consistent internal
one when comparing stones:

| Grade | Sheen strength | Coverage | Continuity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exceptional | Vivid, mirror-bright | Full dome | Travels smoothly, no breaks |
| Fine | Strong, obvious | Most of the dome | Minor interruptions |
| Good | Clearly visible | Partial | Some stutter or unevenness |
| Modest | Faint or narrow | Limited zone | Breaks up noticeably |

<Callout type="tip" title="Compare like with like">
Always judge two candidate stones under the **same light, at the same angle**.
Sheen is dramatically light-dependent, and a flattering shop spotlight can make an
average stone look exceptional.
</Callout>

## Step 2 — Judge the body colour

Under and around the sheen lies the body colour — golden-brown, olive, greenish,
grey, or a deep blue. There is no single "best" colour; it's a matter of taste and
how well the colour supports the sheen. What matters more is **contrast**: a body
colour that lets the golden sheen read clearly is worth more than one that muddies
it.

## Step 3 — Assess the cut

Cut quality in a cabochon is easy to overlook and important to price:

- **Symmetry:** is the dome even and the outline regular?
- **Dome height:** too flat and the sheen weakens; too high and the stone looks
  clumsy. A well-judged dome maximises the effect.
- **Polish:** the sheen relies on a clean, reflective surface. Scratches or a dull
  polish scatter the effect.
- **Orientation:** the single most important cutting decision — the sheen must be
  aligned to the finished face, as covered in [Jewellery &
  settings](/guide/gold-sheen-sapphire-jewelry-and-settings/).

## Step 4 — Weigh size against quality

As with all fine gems, larger stones are rarer and cost more per carat — but never
buy size at the expense of sheen. A small stone with a vivid, even sheen is a
better stone than a large one with a weak, broken one. Because the deposit is
finite, larger high-sheen stones are especially scarce.

## Step 5 — Verify it's natural and unheated

This is non-negotiable for any significant purchase.

- Ask for a report from an **independent gemmological laboratory** (not just the
  seller's own paperwork).
- The report should confirm **natural corundum** and, ideally, note **no
  indication of heating**.
- Keep the report with the stone; it supports both value and resale.

<Callout type="key" title="Certification checklist">
Independent lab · natural corundum confirmed · unheated noted · weight and
measurements match the stone · report number verifiable with the issuing lab.
</Callout>

## Understanding price

There is no fixed price sheet for Gold Sheen Sapphire, and anyone quoting a single
"per-carat" number is oversimplifying. Price is driven by the combination of the
following factors, weighted roughly as shown:

| Price driver | Relative weight | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sheen quality | ●●●●● | Strength, evenness and coverage — the biggest lever |
| Body colour | ●●● | Judged by contrast with the sheen |
| Cut & orientation | ●●● | Symmetry, dome, polish, alignment |
| Size (carat) | ●●● | Rarer large, but never above sheen |
| Scarcity | ●●●● | Depleted source — supply cannot grow |

For a fuller framework and worked examples, see the dedicated [Gold Sheen Sapphire
price guide](/guide/gold-sheen-sapphire-price-guide/).

## Avoiding fakes and misrepresentation

The two risks are outright imitations and honest-sounding misdescription (for
example, a treated or assembled stone sold as natural). Protect yourself by buying
certified material, learning the visual tells, and dealing with sellers who welcome
independent verification. The full method is in [how to spot a fake Gold Sheen
Sapphire](/guide/how-to-spot-fake-gold-sheen-sapphire/).

## A simple buyer's workflow

1. Look at the **sheen** under a single light — strength, coverage, continuity.
2. Check **body colour** and its contrast with the sheen.
3. Inspect the **cut** — symmetry, dome, polish, orientation.
4. Weigh **size** against all of the above.
5. Confirm **certification**: natural, unheated, independently issued.
6. Only then, compare **price** across like-for-like stones.
