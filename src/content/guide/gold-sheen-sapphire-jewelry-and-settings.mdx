A Gold Sheen Sapphire only truly comes alive when it's cut and set to show its
sheen — and worn, where changing light lets the band of gold move. This pillar
covers how the stone is cut, how designers orient and set it, and how to build a
piece around a gem whose beauty is dynamic rather than static. For protecting that
piece over time, see [Care & maintenance](/guide/gold-sheen-sapphire-care-and-maintenance/).

<KeyTakeaways>
- Cut **en cabochon** — a domed surface lets the sheen travel; facets would shatter it.
- **Orientation** is the cutter's key decision and a major driver of value.
- **Metal is a frame**: yellow/rose gold warms the sheen; white metals raise contrast.
- Choose **settings that let light reach the dome** — bezels protect, prongs lift into the light.
- **Rings** show the sheen best (constant motion); matched pairs are a specialist skill.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Why cabochons, not facets

Faceting is designed to bounce light *out* of a transparent stone in bright flashes.
Gold Sheen Sapphire's beauty is a *surface* phenomenon — a reflection that needs an
unbroken, curved face to travel across. Cut it as a **cabochon** — smooth and domed
— and the sheen reads as one continuous band. Facet it, and you shatter the effect
into disconnected glints. This is why virtually every Gold Sheen Sapphire you'll see
is a cabochon.

## Orientation: the cutter's key decision

The single most important choice in cutting is **orientation**. The inclusion planes
that create the sheen run in a specific direction inside the rough. The cutter must
read that direction and align the dome so the finished sheen:

- crosses the **middle** of the face, not an edge;
- travels **cleanly** as the stone rocks;
- covers as much of the dome as possible.

Get this right and a modest stone sings; get it wrong and even fine rough
disappoints. It's a craft decision that directly drives value — see the [buying
guide](/guide/gold-sheen-sapphire-buying-guide/) for how to judge the result.

## Choosing the metal

Metal is a frame for the sheen, and it changes the mood of the stone:

| Metal | Effect on the sheen | Best with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yellow gold | Warms and amplifies the gold | Golden-brown / olive bodies |
| Rose gold | Soft, contemporary warmth | Warm-toned stones |
| White gold / platinum | Cool, high-contrast frame | Bluer or greyer bodies |

The right choice depends on the individual stone's body colour and the sheen's tone.
When possible, view the stone against several metals before deciding.

## Settings that protect and present

A good setting does two jobs: it keeps the stone secure, and it presents the sheen
well.

- **Bezel settings** wrap the girdle in metal — highly protective, clean-lined, and
  excellent for everyday rings. The rim can also frame the sheen neatly.
- **Prong settings** lift the stone into the light and show more of the dome, at the
  cost of slightly more exposure; well-made prongs are a fine choice for pendants and
  dress rings.
- **Half-bezel / partial settings** balance protection and light.

Because the phenomenon depends on light reaching and reflecting off the dome, avoid
settings that shade or crowd the top of the stone.

<Callout type="tip" title="Design principle">
Set the stone so it can catch **changing** light as the wearer moves. A Gold Sheen
Sapphire seen only under one fixed light never shows its best trait.
</Callout>

## Designing by piece type

- **Rings:** the most rewarding — hands move constantly, so the sheen is always
  travelling. Favour protective settings and a well-judged dome height.
- **Pendants & necklaces:** swing and turn against the light beautifully; a slight
  forward tilt in the setting can help the sheen face the viewer.
- **Earrings:** motion is built in; pairs benefit from matched sheen direction and
  body colour.
- **Statement / bespoke pieces:** a single large, well-oriented cabochon can anchor
  an entire design.

## Matching and pairing stones

Because each stone carries its own density and direction of sheen, **matching** two
or more for a pair or a suite is a real skill. When commissioning multi-stone pieces,
discuss sheen direction, body colour and dome profile up front — matched stones cost
more for good reason.
