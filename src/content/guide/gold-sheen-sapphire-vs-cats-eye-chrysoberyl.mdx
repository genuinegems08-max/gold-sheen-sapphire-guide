Chatoyancy — a moving band of light — appears in several gems, and two of the most
admired are Gold Sheen Sapphire and cat's-eye chrysoberyl. Here's how they differ. This
article sits under [Gold Sheen Sapphire vs other
sapphires](/guide/gold-sheen-sapphire-vs-other-sapphires/).

<KeyTakeaways>
- **Cat's-eye chrysoberyl:** a single, razor-sharp **cat's-eye** band; the benchmark chatoyant gem.
- **Gold Sheen Sapphire:** a **broad, golden sheen** that spreads across the dome.
- Different minerals: **corundum (Mohs 9)** vs **chrysoberyl (Mohs 8.5)**.
- Both cut as cabochons; both durable; different looks and markets.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Sharp band vs broad sheen

The clearest difference is the light itself:

- **Cat's-eye chrysoberyl** concentrates reflections from ultra-fine parallel inclusions
  into one **tight, sharp line** that glides across the dome — the classic "cat's eye,"
  often with a milk-and-honey effect.
- **Gold Sheen Sapphire** spreads reflections from platy hematite–ilmenite inclusions
  into a **broad, golden band** — more a moving glow than a single line. See [what Gold
  Sheen Sapphire is](/guide/what-is-gold-sheen-sapphire/).

## Side by side

| | Gold Sheen Sapphire | Cat's-eye chrysoberyl |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mineral | Corundum | Chrysoberyl |
| Mohs hardness | 9 | ~8.5 |
| Effect | Broad golden sheen | Single sharp cat's-eye |
| Colour | Golden/blue/olive bodies | Yellow to greenish, "honey" |
| Source | Single depleted Kenyan deposit | Sri Lanka, others |

## Which to choose

It's a matter of the look you want: the **drama of a single crisp line** (chrysoberyl)
or the **warm, spreading movement** of the sheen (Gold Sheen Sapphire) — plus the latter's
finite, single-source rarity. Both reward being seen in moving light.
